# Muckrakers

The muckrakers were a cohort of reform-minded journalists, writers, and photographers in the Progressive Era who claimed to expose corruption and wrongdoing in established institutions, often through sensationalist publications. Muckraking magazines — notably McClure's of the publisher S. S. McClure — took on corporate monopolies and [[hail_columbia/Термины/Political Machines|Political Machines]], while trying to raise public awareness and anger at urban poverty, unsafe working conditions, prostitution, and child labor. Most of the muckrakers wrote nonfiction, but fictional exposés often had a major impact, too, such as those by Upton Sinclair.

In contrast with objective reporting, the muckrakers saw themselves primarily as reformers and were politically engaged. While the muckrakers continued the investigative exposures and sensational traditions of the older 'yellow' journalism style, they wrote to change society. Their work reached a mass audience as circulation figures of the magazines rose on account of visibility and public interest.

Prominent historical muckrakers include Ida M. Tarbell, Upton Sinclair, and Lincoln Steffens.
